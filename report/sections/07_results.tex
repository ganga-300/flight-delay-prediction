\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Model selection on August}
Table~\ref{tab:august} summarises the 12 candidate runs. All Logistic Regression settings scored
within 0.001 PR-AUC of each other, and the best XGBoost run (depth 5, learning rate 0.1, 16 trees)
was 0.005 lower. Logistic Regression with $C = 0.01$ and balanced class weights was selected. That
two very different model families perform almost identically suggests the limit is the information
in the features rather than model capacity. The August delay rate is 24.6\%, so the selected model
ranks delayed flights about 1.6 times better than random (Fig.~\ref{fig:pr}).

\textbf{Feature importance.} For the tuned XGBoost model, the largest gain comes from scheduled
departure time (\texttt{dep\_hour\_sin}, \texttt{sched\_dep\_hour\_frac}), the previous day's carrier
delay rate, the carrier--departure-time cross and scheduled origin volume. Permutation importance
on August ranks the carrier--departure-time cross, the carrier--route cross, origin daily volume and
departure time highest. Time of day, carrier and recent delays carry most of the signal, matching
the exploratory analysis. Permutation estimates use 2{,}000 August flights and two repeats, so they
are indicative only.

\subsection{Calibration and threshold (September and October)}
The selected model's raw probabilities are far too high (Table~\ref{tab:calib}), as expected from
balanced class weights. Sigmoid calibration fitted on September reduces October ECE from 0.227 to
0.027 and Brier score from 0.192 to 0.136, without changing PR-AUC or ROC-AUC. Isotonic calibration
was slightly worse on October and was not used.

No threshold reached 60\% precision on October, so, as planned, no threshold was set and the
rescheduling rule was not applied. As a diagnostic only, the highest October precision with at
least 20 delayed flights flagged was 51.3\% (158 flights flagged, recall 1.6\%), and no October
flight received a calibrated probability above 0.525.

\subsection{Test results (November--December, evaluated once)}
Table~\ref{tab:test} reports the single evaluation on the sealed test set. PR-AUC is 0.398 against
a delay rate of 0.258, about 1.5 times a random ranking, and ROC-AUC is 0.664. These are close to
the validation values (ROC-AUC 0.67--0.69), so the model generalises to the later period without
collapse. Calibration reduces ECE from 0.204 to 0.077 and Brier score from 0.226 to 0.185. The
calibrated probabilities are, however, too low on average (0.182 predicted vs 0.258 observed;
Fig.~\ref{fig:reliability}), because the calibration was fitted on September, the calmest month
(13.7\% delayed). Threshold-based metrics are not available, because no operating point met the
precision target on October.

\subsection{Discussion}
At the end of the previous day, the dataset offers only the schedule and past outcomes. The largest
drivers of delay, such as weather and same-day propagation, are unavailable. The model therefore
separates riskier from safer flights consistently, but it is rarely confident about an individual
flight, which is why a 60\% precision operating point does not exist. The validation period was
also calmer than both training and test, which affects anything fitted on it, most visibly
calibration.
