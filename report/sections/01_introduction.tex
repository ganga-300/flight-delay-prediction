\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Flight delays propagate through crew rotations, aircraft turnarounds and gate schedules. Airline
operations teams plan next-day buffers (spare crew, spare aircraft, gate assignments) the evening
before, so a calibrated estimate of each flight's delay risk at that point is directly useful. Machine
learning for flight delays is an active field~\cite{carvalho2021relevance}. We
study the problem as binary classification: will a scheduled flight arrive at least 15 minutes
late, the U.S.\ Bureau of Transportation Statistics on-time definition~\cite{bts_ontime}, using
only information available at the end of the previous day?

The setting has two pitfalls. First, many informative fields (actual departure time, departure
delay, taxi and air time) are known only after departure, and rolling delay statistics can easily
include outcomes that were not yet known at prediction time. Either mistake is a form of
leakage~\cite{kaufman2012leakage} and produces offline scores that no deployed system could reach.
Second, delays cluster in time, so evaluation must respect temporal order~\cite{bergmeir2012use}.
Validating on randomly mixed days, or selecting models on the final test set, overstates
performance.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A leakage-safe feature pipeline of 59 schedule, calendar, carrier, airport, congestion and
  past-only historical features. Outcome timing is resolved in a common time zone, and tests verify
  that later records never change earlier features.
  \item A chronological protocol in which each period serves one purpose (fitting, tuning,
  calibration, threshold selection, testing). It is backed by a decision log written before the
  results each decision governs, and by a test set sealed before any analysis and evaluated once.
  \item A comparison of L2 Logistic Regression and XGBoost with probability calibration, reported
  with ranking (PR-AUC, ROC-AUC) and calibration (Brier, ECE) metrics.
  \item A transparent account of what a day-ahead, schedule-only model can and cannot achieve:
  consistent ranking and good calibration, but no 60\%-precision operating point without weather
  information.
\end{itemize}
