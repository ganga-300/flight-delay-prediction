\section{Dataset and Data Quality}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Source and target}
The dataset, provided for the course, is the Kaggle \emph{Flights} table: all 336{,}776 flights
departing the three New York City airports (EWR, JFK, LGA) in 2013, with 21 columns of scheduled
and actual times, carrier, route and distance. The target is $y = 1$ if the arrival delay is at
least 15 minutes, the U.S.\ Bureau of Transportation Statistics on-time convention~\cite{bts_ontime},
and $y = 0$ otherwise. Flights without an arrival-delay value (cancelled, or departed with no
arrival record) are excluded, so the model estimates the probability of a delay given that the
flight operates and arrives. Cancellations and diversions are a separate prediction problem.

\subsection{Chronological split and sealed test set}
The data are split by date: training January--August (217{,}727 labelled flights, 26.4\%
delayed), validation September--October (55{,}628, 15.7\%) and test November--December (53{,}991,
25.8\%). Before any analysis, the November--December rows were moved out of the development data
(the full file's SHA-256 was recorded). Every notebook, feature build and training script reads a
January--October file through a loader that rejects later dates. The test rows were read once, in
the final evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Data quality}
On the January--October development data (281{,}373 rows) there are no duplicate rows, no
duplicate flight keys and no error-level violations: all clock times are well formed, and
departure and arrival delays agree with actual minus scheduled times once midnight wrap-around is
allowed. Missing values are structured rather than random. All actual-time fields are missing
together for the 6{,}997 cancelled flights (2.5\%), 1{,}021 flights departed without an
arrival-delay value (0.4\%), and the aircraft tail number is missing only for cancelled flights.
Because that missingness reveals the outcome, the tail number is not used as a feature. Actual
departure and arrival times are post-departure information: departure delay alone reaches a
single-feature ROC-AUC of 0.898 against the target, so these fields are excluded from all
features.

\subsection{Exploratory analysis on the training period only}
All exploratory statistics that informed feature design use January--August only. The delay rate
rises steadily through the day, from 10.8\% for 05:00 departures to 40.5\% at 21:00. Thursday is
the worst day (31.0\%) and Saturday the best (18.5\%). Carriers differ widely (ExpressJet 35.3\%,
American 20.3\%), and the airport effect is not uniform across carriers (e.g.\ Endeavor 19.1\% at
EWR vs 29.5\% at JFK), which motivates carrier--airport interaction features. The daily delay rate
has a lag-1 autocorrelation of 0.356, but lags 2 and 7 are near zero (0.041 and 0.021): yesterday's
delays are informative, but persistence beyond that is weak. Monthly delay rates vary between
22.7\% and 32.2\% within the training period.
